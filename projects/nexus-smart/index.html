Nexus Smart

Operational software for BRT stations — designed around what operators need to act fast.

Overview

Bus terminals are complex environments. Vehicles arrive late. Platforms change. Schedules shift. Thousands of people move through the same physical space every day. Behind it all is a control layer most passengers never see. When it works, the system disappears. When it fails, everyone knows.

Research in Bogotá

Before designing anything, I spent time in TransMilenio terminals watching operators work. They split attention constantly between the screen and the station floor. The information they needed was spread across systems and lagged behind what was actually happening. By the time data appeared on-screen, the window to act had often closed. The problem wasn't more information — it was timing and hierarchy.

Door Control Interface

A dashboard shows data. This had to drive action. Every second an operator spends interpreting a screen is a second not spent on the station floor. The interface was designed to reduce that cost: the right information visible by default, with clear paths to what to do next — nothing to parse.

Operational Routines

Every workflow was built around one constraint: visibility has to earn its place. The state model for each gate — open, closed, manual, error, anti-panic, no network, emergency — was designed so an operator could read the situation in a glance and act. What showed by default and what required a deliberate action was the most important call in the system.

Interactions in Motion

A well-designed operational system disappears into the work. That was the target. If the interface needed to explain itself, it had failed. If an operator could act without thinking about it, it had succeeded.

In the Field

TransMilenio terminals are high-throughput environments. Design decisions that look reasonable on a screen behave very differently when an operator is managing several thousand commuters across a peak shift. Field observation shaped everything.

What It Delivers

A system operators can read in a glance and act on without interpretation. The interface mirrors the station itself — what's exceptional surfaces immediately, what's routine stays out of the way. Repetitive work is handled through routines. Emergencies have one-step overrides.
